# Project Logo Rights Research

Checked: 2026-08-14

This memo records the rights screen, provenance, and use controls for replacing
OpenAI-owned project-branding and package icons. It is an engineering risk
assessment, not legal advice. It does not clear the project name or provide a
comprehensive trademark search.

## Decision

Use a properly attributed Tux derivative with independently authored
prosthesis geometry. Do not copy, trace, crop, rotate, recolor, distort, splice,
or otherwise transform the OpenAI Blossom or another OpenAI icon. Do not assume
that a small or partial use is permitted.

The baseline screen for future candidates remains conservative: reject artwork
that incorporates OpenAI artwork, copies protectable Blossom expression, reads
as an OpenAI logo variant when viewed as a complete mark, or implies OpenAI
source or sponsorship.

Future clean-sheet exploration may use concepts such as packaging, conversion,
bridging, containers, terminal apertures, modular assembly, and a Linux
destination. Those concepts are not a safe harbor from provenance and
whole-mark review.

The operator approved one exact artifact after a separate provenance,
expression, and whole-mark review:

- canonical file: `assets/chatgpt-linux-project-logo.svg`
- SHA-256:
  `0af87e4126277df510b84d9df858e68446ee119384ed740d1617363a66f585ae`
- decision date: 2026-08-14

For only that digest, the
[artifact-specific acceptance rule](#artifact-specific-acceptance-rule)
supersedes this memo's earlier zero-recognizable-allusion inference. It does
not authorize copying OpenAI artwork, publish the prosthesis as a separate
mark, relax the baseline for future artwork, claim OpenAI approval, or remove
the project's unofficial and non-endorsement disclosures.

Recognition of an intentional reference is not treated as source or
sponsorship confusion by itself. The approved artifact is assessed from its
complete commercial impression: a Tux-dominant, asymmetric, full-color mark
with an original terminal-eye prosthesis.

## Questions kept separate

The rights screen answers three distinct questions:

1. **Use or modification of OpenAI artwork.** The approved SVG contains no
   OpenAI path, coordinate set, contour, crop, trace, raster fragment, or
   embedded asset. The Blossom was studied as a visual reference, not used as
   construction material.
2. **Reproduction of protected expression.** The approved prosthesis uses an
   abstract geometric construction grammar but expresses it through newly
   authored heptagonal paths, a partial four-sector arrangement, a functional
   terminal eye, and its fit within a Tux portrait.
3. **Similarity or confusion.** The finished composite must be assessed in its
   entirety and at its real display sizes. Isolating an evocative drafting
   technique is not a whole-mark commercial-impression test.

This decision does not depend on a copied element being minor,
noncommerciality, redrawing, a particular petal count, or fair use. The
project's position is that no protectable Blossom expression was incorporated
and that the complete mark does not imply OpenAI source or sponsorship.

## Confirmed requirements

1. OpenAI's [Design Guidelines](https://openai.com/brand/) apply to third
   parties using OpenAI trademarks or copyrighted work. They identify the
   OpenAI name and logo, ChatGPT, GPT, and other OpenAI trademarks as OpenAI
   property. Their usage terms define **Marks** to include names, logos, icons,
   and design elements.
2. The guidelines say not to use the Blossom as primary branding, imply
   endorsement, confuse users about sponsorship, make an OpenAI logo more
   prominent than one's own, modify it, incorporate it into one's own branding
   or trademark, or design a similar logo. A permitted OpenAI-logo use must
   relate directly to OpenAI services, use the logo exactly as provided, follow
   the guidelines and usage terms, and acknowledge OpenAI ownership.
3. Permission under the guidelines is conditional, non-exclusive,
   non-transferable, reviewable, revocable, and subject to future guideline
   changes. OpenAI reserves the right to review uses and require changes. The
   page directs logo-permission requests and questions beyond its stated cases
   to `partnercomms@openai.com`.
4. OpenAI's [Terms of Use](https://openai.com/policies/terms-of-use/), published
   and effective January 1, 2026, say that OpenAI's name and logo may be used
   only in accordance with the Design Guidelines. They also require the user
   to have the rights needed for Input, make the user responsible for Content,
   and require human evaluation of Output before use or sharing.
5. Under those Terms, the user owns Output as between the user and OpenAI, to
   the extent permitted by law, but Output may not be unique. That assignment
   does not grant rights held by another party or override OpenAI's rules for
   its Marks.
6. OpenAI's [Service Terms](https://openai.com/policies/service-terms/), updated
   June 12, 2026, exclude trademark claims based on commercial use of Output
   from the stated API and Enterprise output indemnities. The exclusions also
   cover cases where a customer knew or should have known that Output was
   infringing or likely to infringe. Generating a prototype with an OpenAI
   image model therefore does not clear it for use as a mark.
7. [17 U.S.C. § 102(b)](https://www.copyright.gov/title17/92chap1.html#102)
   excludes ideas, procedures, processes, systems, methods of operation,
   concepts, principles, and discoveries from copyright protection. The U.S.
   Copyright Office's
   [Circular 33](https://www.copyright.gov/circs/circ33.pdf) likewise explains
   that protection for an illustration of an idea or method does not extend to
   the underlying idea or method.
8. The U.S. Copyright Office's
   [Fair Use FAQ](https://www.copyright.gov/help/faq/faq-fairuse.html) says there
   is no fixed amount or percentage of a work that may be used without
   permission. It also says that changing another's work does not make it one's
   own without the owner's consent. The approved artifact does not rely on a
   fair-use defense for copied artwork.
9. The USPTO's official
   [likelihood-of-confusion guidance](https://www.uspto.gov/trademarks/search/likelihood-confusion)
   says marks need not be identical to be confusingly similar. Similarity may
   arise from appearance, meaning, or overall commercial impression, including
   a shared dominant design element; related goods or services increase the
   concern. Marks are considered in their entireties.
10. The U.S. Copyright Office's
    [AI copyrightability report](https://www.copyright.gov/ai/Copyright-and-Artificial-Intelligence-Part-2-Copyrightability-Report.pdf)
    says copyright does not extend to purely AI-generated material or material
    with insufficient human control over expressive elements. Human-authored
    creative modifications may be protectable case by case.

## Official Blossom vector availability

Checked: 2026-08-13

OpenAI's current [Design Guidelines](https://openai.com/brand/) identify the
mark as the OpenAI Blossom, display a “Download logos” control, and directly
embed an official
[`Blossom_Light.svg`](https://images.ctfassets.net/kftzwdyauwt9/3hUGLn3ypllZ0oa01qOYVq/28e8188e6f11b84c3e876569d492734f/Blossom_Light.svg)
from the page's managed asset CDN.

Availability is not permission to derive another mark from that vector. The
official SVG may support provenance and whole-mark comparison, but it must not
be traced, embedded, transformed, uploaded as image-model input, or used as a
construction template for this project. Mirroring the Blossom, reconstructing
its petal-to-center paths, replacing its center polygon, or rasterizing it as
an image-model reference would not satisfy this screen.

## Approved artifact creation record

The canonical SVG starts with Garrett LeSage's CC0 vector redraw of Larry
Ewing's Tux, refined by IFo Hancroft. The prosthesis paths were authored anew
around a regular heptagon after viewing the Blossom as a reference. No OpenAI
vector or raster artwork was incorporated or transformed.

The rejected workflow was to take the Blossom vector and replace its center.
The accepted workflow plotted new SVG paths around a regular heptagon. That
choice produces different side directions, departure points, bends,
intersections, and arc endpoints from the Blossom's sixfold construction. The
finished expression comprises:

- a full-color, square Tux headshot;
- Tux's viewer-right eye replaced by a filled blue regular heptagon containing
  a mirrored terminal prompt;
- four equal-width, white, black-outlined modules occupying four of seven
  possible sectors along one side of the face;
- an independently designed rhomboid closing the first module;
- an asymmetric assembly fitted to Tux's forehead, eye, and cheek; and
- a yellow beak overlapping the lower assembly.

The design does not reproduce the Blossom's complete six-part knot, central
void, full radial sequence, standalone silhouette, or monochrome presentation.
Its original expression also includes the facial fit, occlusion, color,
shading, Tux silhouette, functional terminal eye, and partial arrangement.

Preserve the exact canonical SVG bytes and their metadata. Preserve this
construction record, the Tux source record, export commands, raster-export
digests, selection record, and final visual audit with the maintained asset.
Raster exports are derivatives of the canonical SVG; they are not independent
logo sources.

The immutable SVG description still contains the draft-era phrase “Temporary
fit study.” The exact-digest final selection and approval recorded here
supersede that phrase. Do not edit the approved SVG merely to update its
metadata.

The concept exploration used ChatGPT Image 2 to generate raster candidates from
text prompts. The operator selected the final raster direction, then the final
SVG paths were constructed manually from the Tux vector geometry and original
heptagonal prosthesis geometry rather than automatically tracing that raster.
The exact approved SVG digest binds the resulting human construction. Its
embedded metadata preserves the underlying Tux record; this memo supplies the
project-specific construction, review, and revision record without changing the
approved bytes.

## Artifact-specific acceptance rule

The following gates apply only to the complete SVG with SHA-256
`0af87e4126277df510b84d9df858e68446ee119384ed740d1617363a66f585ae`:

1. **Provenance:** the file contains no copied OpenAI asset, path, coordinate
   set, trace, crop, or raster fragment. Preserve the human SVG construction
   record and Tux provenance.
2. **Expression:** the file contains no corresponding Blossom contour,
   complete sixfold interlock, central-negative-space topology, or standalone
   Blossom silhouette. Do not reject abstract band-and-polygon construction
   ideas merely because their allusion is intentional.
3. **Commercial impression:** at actual icon and README sizes, the complete
   mark reads first as Tux with a blue terminal prosthesis, not as OpenAI's logo
   or an official OpenAI Linux distribution.
4. **Use controls:** publish only the complete Tux composite. Do not export the
   prosthesis as a submark, create a standalone monochrome variant, remove the
   attribution, or omit conspicuous unofficial and non-endorsement language.
5. **Evidence-based escalation:** stop for a concrete copied element, a failed
   whole-mark comparison, new controlling primary-source language, an OpenAI
   objection or enforcement demand, or a separate unresolved project-name
   issue. The already-reviewed intentional allusion is not by itself a new
   blocker.

This rule supersedes the earlier blanket rejection of any recognizable Blossom
petal, loop, hub, topology, or negative-space pattern for this exact digest
only.

| Dominant subject | Standalone abstract corporate symbol | Recognizable Tux portrait |
| Organization | Complete, centered six-part radial knot | Four partial sectors on one side of a face |

| Silhouette | Symmetrical rosette | Asymmetric penguin head and beak |
| Color | Monochrome | Black, white, yellow, blue, and shaded Tux artwork |
| Meaning | OpenAI source identity | Unofficial community Linux adaptation and interoperability |
| Independent use | Functions by itself as the OpenAI mark | Prosthesis must never be published alone |

At 512 px, the approved asset reads as Tux with an asymmetric terminal-eye
prosthesis. At 32 px, the Tux head, natural eye, yellow beak, and blue
replacement eye remain the dominant cues; the four modules compress into
headgear rather than a standalone rosette.

The products are closely related, so surrounding use remains important. The
repository and packages must conspicuously say that the project is unofficial
and is not affiliated with, endorsed by, sponsored by, or supported by OpenAI.
A disclaimer would not rescue a confusing logo, but it reinforces the
distinct complete-mark impression and prevents the intentional allusion from
being presented as approval.

## General candidate screen

Future candidates must pass all of these checks unless the operator records a
new artifact-specific decision:

- The concept and geometry are independently authored. No OpenAI logo or app
  icon is embedded, traced, cropped, transformed, used as an image-model input,
  or used as a construction template.
- The complete mark's dominant silhouette, internal rhythm, negative space,
  meaning, and commercial impression do not present it as an OpenAI logo
  variant or imply OpenAI source, sponsorship, endorsement, or support.
- Distinctness does not depend mainly on color, texture, a Linux badge, a
  package badge, or surrounding text added to otherwise OpenAI-like geometry.
- Every third-party element has documented provenance and permission covering
  its use. Tux-derived artwork retains all required credits.
- The final SVG records its source, derivative geometry, original geometry,
  authorship, and revisions. It is reconstructed from selected source geometry
  rather than automatically traced from a generated raster.
- The complete full-color asset remains recognizable at 32, 64, 256, and
  README-hero sizes. A separate monochrome mark is neither required nor
  authorized for the approved artifact.
- A rights reviewer can explain its independent construction and provenance
  without describing a transformation of OpenAI artwork.

A clean-sheet workflow does not save a result that is confusingly similar.
Conversely, intentional recognition of a visual reference does not fail a
candidate automatically; use provenance, expression, and whole-mark analysis.

## Tux and cybernetic-portrait boundary

The selected direction combines a permitted Tux head with original
cybernetic-prosthesis geometry. The high-level idea of replacing much of one
side of a character's face with a prosthesis is used without copying Picard,
Locutus, Borg hardware, or a Star Trek composition.

### Project controls

- Use the canonical Tux artwork as the only third-party image source. Preserve
  the recognizable Tux head, eye, beak, and dual-tone facial contours.
- Retain source provenance and credit Larry Ewing and The GIMP. Because the
  LeSage/Hancroft SVG redraw is the vector source, credit Garrett LeSage and IFo
  Hancroft as well.
- Do not upload, trace, or copy Picard, Locutus, Borg, Blossom, or other
  third-party artwork. A visual comparison reference is not construction
  material.
- Do not copy characteristic Star Trek hardware, eye devices, face-entering
  cables, character composition, contours, or insignia. Do not use Star Trek
  character names in public logo metadata; they were ideation shorthand, not
  source material in the finished expression.
- For future concepts, keep any prosthesis on the viewer-right upper portion of
  Tux's face and build it from original open-ended paths with controlled band
  widths, gaps, and angular joins. Reject unrelated circuit-board decoration.
- Apply the complete-mark test to any prosthesis geometry. Do not isolate an
  evocative component and treat it as an independent project mark.

## Conservative process controls

These controls are conservative project policy, not quoted OpenAI rules:

- Do not prompt an image model to reproduce, reinterpret, deconstruct, or
  retain a portion of an OpenAI logo. Use text-only prompts for independent
  concepts and explicitly exclude third-party logos.
- Keep necessary, accurate textual references to the official ChatGPT product
  separate from the project's visual mark.
- Retain candidate prompts, raster prototypes, selection records, SVG source,
  export commands, hashes, and final visual audits as provenance evidence.
- Re-run the rights screen on the finished SVG and small-size exports.
- Do not infer that clean-sheet work automatically passes the complete-mark
  similarity screen, or that an intentional allusion automatically fails it.

## Residual risk and unresolved questions

1. OpenAI has not provided written permission or approval for the approved
   artwork. OpenAI may interpret or change its discretionary brand policy and
   require a future change. This decision acknowledges that residual risk; it
   is not a guarantee against an objection or enforcement demand.
2. The separate question whether `ChatGPT for Linux`, `chatgpt`, or related
   package identifiers are permissible project or product names is not cleared
   by approving the artwork. It requires its own trademark or contract review
   or written clearance.
3. The creation handoff and exact-artifact approval did not identify which
   OpenAI account agreement governed candidate generation, and the approval did
   not make that absence a stop condition. Treat it as a residual record gap for
   this exact artifact. Future candidate work must record its governing
   agreement before generation. If the
   [OpenAI Services Agreement](https://openai.com/policies/services-agreement/)
   applies, its January 1, 2026 version requires express prior written
   permission in each instance before one party includes the other party's
   name or logo on websites, media, or marketing materials.
4. This memo is not a comprehensive federal, state, or common-law trademark
   clearance search. A broader search should confirm that the original mark
   does not conflict with marks owned by parties other than OpenAI.
5. This is a reasoned project-policy and IP-risk determination, not legal
   advice, a claim of zero risk, or a substitute for qualified counsel.
